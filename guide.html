What a good idea looks like

Problems, in the words of the person who has them.
Automate the repetitive · School Apparel
"In season, orders arrive by email, phone, and the web store, and every one gets re-keyed into the order system by hand. Two people, most of the day, and the typos come back as returns."
Names the pain, the season, the headcount, and what a mistake costs. Coop can work with this.
Replace what does not fit · School Apparel
"The system that runs our core order process fails at basic things and does not talk properly to our web store. We work around it every day and the workarounds are the process now."
Sometimes the answer is our own build, or a different tool. Say where the current one fights you; the Furnace decides which.
Arm the reps · AllRec
"A rep gets asked 'why you, and when can I have it?' and answers from memory. Who we are up against, what we do better, and this week's real delivery time all exist. They live in five people's heads and a whiteboard."
Revenue idea, moat idea. The knowledge is the raw material; putting the competitor landscape, our advantages, and live lead times in every rep's hands is the build.
Forecast from our own data · Kinetico SA
"Ask two people what a new customer costs us and you get two answers, far apart. We spend real money on advertising every month and set next month's budget on a number nobody trusts."
Measure cost per customer one way, by channel, forecast it forward, and let the next dollar of ad spend go where it works. The data is already in our systems.
Connect what we have · Kinetico SA
"A web lead comes in and nothing tells us how long before a human answers it. Our lead system and our dispatch system both know part of the story. Neither knows the whole thing."
Two systems that already work, not talking. A text within a minute and a timestamp on every handoff is a small build with a big number behind it.
Works for every company · HoldCo and all three
"Every month, each company keys the same scorecard numbers into the same spreadsheets by hand, and someone at Enduring keys them in again. Three companies, one holdco, the same afternoon lost four times."
An idea that pays once per company is worth three of the others. Tag it Multiple in the Screener and it moves up the queue on reuse alone.

Six kinds of idea, one thing in common: each says what hurts, not what to build. Say what hurts. We will figure out the rest.

Inside the Furnace

For Enduring Co, and for anyone curious what happens to an idea once it goes in.

Stage 1: The Screener

Every idea, without exception, ten minutes. Five red-line questions: is there a real business problem, can we estimate the profit impact, is there a named owner, can it run on what we already have, and can sensitive data stay where it belongs. One "no" must be resolved before anything moves. Two stop it. A declared run-rate number comes out of this stage, and it is a number Coop will later be held to.

Stage 2: The Pressure Test

For anything over the threshold: more than two weeks of build, any new recurring cost, sensitive data, or more than one company. Nine categories scored and weighted to 100. Plus two things kept beside the score, never inside it: does the company hit a growth ceiling without this, and does it make the customer harder to lose.

The queue, in plain words

One list for the whole portfolio. Growth-blocking work goes first regardless of score. Then savings beat revenue of the same size, because savings reach profit whole and revenue only at margin. Then declared dollars. Then the deeper customer moat. Coop can override the order, and when he does, the reason is logged.

Value, counted

Every project past the screener carries its declared number, a go-live date, and a ramp. That projects what it should contribute this fiscal year and the next two. Each quarter, actuals go in beside the projection. Variance is honest by design: only the quarters that have happened are compared.

Who does what

  • Portfolio leaders drop ideas, forge them, comment, and watch the strip. Each sees only their own company.
  • Coop screens, scores, ranks, builds with Brian, and enters the quarterly actuals. Only Coop finalizes a stage.
  • Brian and Jory can draft and edit screeners and pressure tests, so a decision is never waiting on one calendar.
  • Anyone at Enduring Co can read the Furnace and see every company's list.

Five questions people ask

Who can see my ideas?

You and your company's leadership, plus Enduring Co. The other portfolio companies cannot see your list, and you cannot see theirs. That is enforced by the database, not by politeness.

What if my idea is not about technology?

Drop it anyway. It stays on your list as a plain idea with its tags, and Enduring Co can pick it up from there. The Forge and the Furnace are only for builds.

How long does it take?

The screener happens within five business days of your Forge. A pressure test, when one is needed, takes weeks because it involves people and numbers. Builds run thirty to sixty days once they start.

I forged it, then thought of something. Can I add to it?

Yes. Comment on the card. You can also open the Forge answers again with the gear button to reread what you sent.

Why can't I see the score?

Scores compare projects across companies, and that comparison is Enduring Co's job. What you see is the decision, the reason, and where it sits on the line. That is the part that affects you.
